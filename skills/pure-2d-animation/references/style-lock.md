# Historical Storybook 2D Style Lock

Read this file for every conversion performed by the skill.

## Core visual identity

- Locked visual language: richly rendered South Asian historical/spiritual storybook-comic illustration, derived from the approved portrait, narrative-scene, character-sheet, and bilingual-spread references.
- Use clean dark-brown-to-black tapered ink outlines. Exterior silhouettes are firmer; internal facial, fabric, and material lines are thinner and selective.
- Adult characters use believable anatomy with moderate caricature for readability and drama.
- Faces use a cel-plus-soft 2D treatment: warm local skin color, approximately three to five controlled value groups, crisp feature contours, selective soft transitions on cheeks and nose, and restrained highlight accents. They may include eyelids, nostrils, cheek structure, wrinkles, beard strands, and age cues, but must remain clearly hand-illustrated rather than photographic.
- Expressions may be strongly theatrical when required by the story: widened eyes, angled brows, tears, open mouths, pointing gestures, fear, anger, grief, suspicion, authority, or humor.
- Preserve each source character's emotional role and relative intensity. Do not enlarge eyes or exaggerate emotion unless the source narrative supports it.
- Hair and beards use grouped illustrated strands and controlled internal linework, not photorealistic individual hairs.
- Period clothing uses flowing nested fold shapes, decorative borders, embroidery, jewelry, turbans, beads, saris, monastic robes, and culturally appropriate accessories with readable material differences.
- Favor the locked jewel-tone family where appropriate: saffron, ochre, maroon, purple, turquoise, royal blue, warm cream, deep brown, and luminous gold. Use cool blue, blue-gray, or green atmospheric fields to separate the warm figures.
- Use layered digital coloring: a clear local base color, two or more controlled shadow/highlight levels, and selective soft blending where it supports volume.
- Metals, jewels, coins, polished wood, and ornaments may use crisp highlights, reflected color, and luminous accents, but must remain painted/inked 2D artwork rather than CGI.
- Foreground subjects are crisp and detailed. Midground and distant architecture may use reduced linework, atmospheric haze, controlled soft focus, or depth blur.
- Light may include warm glow, diffused sunlight, rain atmosphere, soft bloom, and luminous haze when motivated by the scene.
- For illustrated page spreads, keep text areas calm and legible; let imagery frame or anchor the text without competing with it.

## Reference-derived rendering rules

- Calm figures retain natural adult eyes, restrained mouths, and composed brows. Distressed or comic figures may use wider eyes, stronger brow arcs, tears, and open mouths, but exaggeration must follow the source emotion rather than becoming a default face style.
- Preserve recognizably South Asian facial variety, age, body type, costume class, religious role, and historical identity. Do not homogenize characters into one generic face.
- Gold jewelry and royal props use clean outlined shapes, warm yellow-orange bases, small bright highlights, and limited reflected color—not metallic 3D shaders.
- Rain, tears, haze, sun glow, and foliage are explicit illustrated layers. Weather affects the palette and atmosphere without softening the focal character's contours.
- Foreground figures and important props are crisp, saturated, and higher contrast. Architecture, foliage, crowds, and distant terrain progressively lose line density, contrast, and saturation.
- Large empty or softly modeled regions are intentional narrative space. Preserve them when they support text, breath, contemplation, or page design.

## Four locked layout modes

1. **Narrative scene:** one readable dramatic beat; clear eyelines and gestures; layered groups; culturally accurate architecture and props; controlled theatrical expression.
2. **Character portrait:** waist-up or three-quarter adult figure; graceful silhouette; richly rendered costume and jewelry; crisp subject against painterly weather or architectural depth.
3. **Character sheet:** stable identity across front, three-quarter, profile, back, expressions, pose, palette, and accessories; clean cream or white presentation ground; precise labels and orderly panels.
4. **Illustrated/bilingual spread:** exact page geometry and text; calm low-detail text-safe zones; story art concentrated below, beside, or framing the copy; background blur/haze may support legibility but never obscure focal figures.

## Composition and narrative staging

- Favor clear story beats, readable gestures, strong eye lines, and foreground/midground/background separation.
- Group scenes should maintain distinct silhouettes and recognizable roles even when characters overlap.
- Use richer detail and contrast at the narrative focal point; reduce saturation, contrast, and detail behind text or distant action.
- Bilingual or book-layout inputs must preserve page split, text columns, reading order, margins, and all supplied wording unless the user asks for changes.
- Preserve cultural and historical cues from the source instead of substituting generic fantasy elements.

## Boundaries

- No photorealistic skin, pores, camera-photo texture, or live-action appearance.
- No 3D render, CGI, plastic character surfaces, game-engine lighting, or 2.5D cutout look.
- No flat minimalist icon style, chibi style, children's sticker style, or vector infographic treatment unless requested.
- Avoid uncontrolled airbrush, muddy gradients, over-smoothed faces, excessive bloom, or blur covering foreground characters.
- Avoid generic anime facial proportions unless present in the source.
- Avoid using oversized eyes as a universal style marker. Eye size must remain adult and source-matched except for narratively justified comic shock or fear.
- Avoid generic fantasy clothing, architecture, ornaments, or symbols when the source specifies Indian or Buddhist historical culture.
- Avoid changing age, ethnicity, identity, costume class, religious role, or historical setting.
- Do not mutate supplied text, names, punctuation, diacritics, or bilingual page structure.

## Approval checklist

- Narrative meaning, characters, roles, actions, relationships, costumes, props, composition, and aspect ratio are preserved.
- Clean inked outlines and readable silhouettes unify all foreground characters.
- Faces are hand-illustrated, adult, expressive, and role-appropriate; they are neither photorealistic nor simplified into flat symbols.
- Emotional intensity matches the source: dramatic faces remain dramatic, calm faces remain calm.
- Eye size, facial proportions, age, and identity remain appropriate to the source unless exaggeration is narratively intentional.
- The selected layout mode is followed consistently; portrait, scene, character-sheet, and page-spread conventions are not mixed accidentally.
- Palette uses coherent warm skin and jewel-tone focal colors with cooler or quieter atmospheric support where appropriate.
- Skin, hair, and beards use controlled illustrated modeling and grouped detail without photographic texture.
- Clothing, folds, embroidery, jewelry, metals, wood, and props have readable illustrated material differences.
- Lighting and highlights enrich the scene without making it look rendered in 3D.
- Foreground subjects remain crisp; atmospheric haze or blur is restricted to midground/distant layers and never obscures the focal action.
- Background architecture and crowds belong to the same storybook world and support, rather than overpower, the focal subjects or text.
- All supplied text, punctuation, diacritics, columns, margins, and page divisions remain intact unless changes were requested.
- No photorealism, live-action texture, CGI, 3D, 2.5D cutout look, generic anime, chibi, plastic surfaces, new logos, signatures, or watermark.

## Focused correction clauses

For faces that became too flat or generic:

```text
Restore the locked historical storybook face treatment: believable adult anatomy,
clean ink contours, controlled cheek/nose/eye modeling, age cues, and grouped beard
or hair strands. Keep the result hand-illustrated and expressive, not photorealistic.
Preserve identity, gaze, emotion, and narrative role.
```

For expressions that lost the source drama:

```text
Match the source emotional intensity exactly. Restore the original eyelid openness,
eye direction, brow angle, mouth shape, tears, tension, and gesture. Dramatic
caricature is allowed when supported by the source; preserve adult proportions and
avoid generic anime or chibi features.
```

For materials that look flat or generic:

```text
Add controlled illustrated material rendering to period fabrics, embroidery,
jewelry, coins, metal, polished wood, and props using layered color, crisp highlights,
and selective soft blending. Keep clean ink outlines and avoid CGI or plastic shine.
```

For foregrounds that became soft or backgrounds that compete with the story:

```text
Keep the focal characters and narrative props crisp, outlined, and high-contrast.
Reduce linework, saturation, contrast, and detail progressively in the midground and
distant background; use only controlled atmospheric haze or soft focus away from the
focal action and text.
```

For text or page-layout drift:

```text
Restore every supplied word, diacritic, punctuation mark, line order, column, margin,
and page division exactly. Keep the illustration outside text-safe areas and do not
redesign the typography unless explicitly requested.
```
